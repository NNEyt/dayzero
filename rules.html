⏱️ Teach it in 2 minutes

  1. It's a power cut. You're neighbours running the Mill Lane community hall for a long, cold weekend. Everyone is on the same team.
  2. Everyone has a role (📻 Theo, 📦 Ines, ✚ Pavel, 🏠 Marta…) and sees different cards. Some are verified, some are just rumours. Say what you know out loud.
  3. Each chapter: read the story, read your cards, talk, settle any big decision, then each role picks its actions. The game shows what happened.
  4. Problems have clocks ⭕. Each chapter, an unsolved clock fills a little. When one fills, something bad happens.
  5. Win stars for getting the hall's Hub running (Warmth, Water, Information), keeping people healthy, and keeping the street together. Don't let Health or Cohesion hit 0.

That's all you need. The game teaches the rest with 💡 tips as they come up, and your first decision arrives within about two minutes.

🕯️ What Day Zero is

A co-operative story game for 1–8 players (best with 3–6) around one shared screen: a TV, laptop or tablet. Kelderwick has lost power. A Full weekend is 7 chapters (about 60–75 min); a Quick Night is 4 chapters (about 30 min). It ends with the Mill Lane Chronicle, a newspaper front page about your game.

📺 Setup

Open the game, type everyone's names, pick Full weekend or Quick Night and press ▶ Play. Choose how screens work under ⚙️ Game options → Screens:

TV only default

Everything happens on one screen. Role cards are shown on the TV for the whole table. Easiest; no phones needed.

TV + phone briefings

Each chapter, players scan a QR code on the TV to read their private cards on their own phone. No connection is made; you still choose actions and vote on the TV.

TV + phone controllers beta

Phones scan a QR code in the TV lobby and become controllers: private cards, secret goal, your own actions and secret votes. Needs internet on every device. Anyone without a phone plays on the TV, and if the link fails the TV offers Play without phones.

A town seed (like K7Q2) decides which rumours are true, which bridge floods, the surprise cards and the goals. Leave it blank for a new town, or share …/?seed=K7Q2 so another table gets the same one.

🎭 Roles

Each role has its own cards and its own actions. With 1–4 players, the four core roles are shared out: one player plays all four, two players take two each, and with three players one person takes two. With 5–8 players, extra roles join in this order.

RoleWhat they do
📻 Theo, SignalRadio: checks rumours and makes broadcasts, using Signal.
📦 Ines, QuartermasterVan journeys and deliveries, using Fuel. Owns the stores.
✚ Pavel, MedicMedical help, using limited Medkits.
🏠 Marta, LocalContacts and local knowledge: knows who has keys and who to ask.
👟 Kit, Runner 🧒Kid role (about 7–11): one job a chapter from big buttons. Fast legs, no costs.
🫖 Bea, Host 🧒Kid role: one kind job a chapter (tea, stories, blankets). No costs.
🔧 Dev, MechanicFixes things with tools, not Fuel: generator, pump, radio mast.
🏛️ Grace, LiaisonOfficial channels: the council, the warden, the depot gate.

Tick 🧒 next to a younger player's name. With 5+ players they get Kit or Bea first. Kid roles have short, simple cards, no rumours, and never see Insider or sabotage text. Bigger tables start a little leaner (5+ players: Supplies −1; 7+: Fuel −1).

🔁 How a round (chapter) works

  1. Story. This chapter's coordinator (the screen names them; it rotates every chapter) reads the story aloud.
  2. Role cards. Each player reads their own cards (on the TV, or privately on a phone). Some are verified, some are rumours.
  3. Council. Share what you know. The screen shows this round's aim, any decision, the problems and their clocks, the town's resources, and sometimes a 🔀 Crossroads card: a small surprise the named player decides on the spot.
  4. Decision. Settle the big question by secret vote, or together on screen. Each decision belongs to a role, who has the final say.
  5. Actions. Each role picks its actions: one in the first chapter, two after that (kid roles always one). They all happen together at the end, so if one action needs another (unlock the stairs, then climb), choose both.
  6. Results, then time passes. See what worked and what was blocked. Unsolved problem clocks tick; a full clock causes harm. ↩️ Echo cards later show consequences of earlier choices.

Resources: the town has Supplies, Health, Cohesion and Trust (1–6); the roles spend Fuel, Signal, Medkits and Contacts. The ⏳ Crisis meter rises every chapter and whenever a problem's clock fills. If it fills up, the game jumps straight to the final chapter.

🗳️ Secret votes

On by default with 2+ players. On a big decision, talk it through, then pass the device round (or vote on your phone in controller mode). Everyone votes privately, then the votes are revealed together.

  • The majority decides. Ties go to the role that owns the decision.
  • If 3 or more of you all agree: Cohesion +1.
  • You can always choose decide together on this screen instead.

🤫 Private goals

On by default. After Chapter 1, each player privately gets something they're quietly hoping for, like “Finish with Health 4 or more” or “Hillside must not be turned away”. Keep it to yourself and steer the table towards it. Goals are revealed and scored in the Chronicle.

🕵️ The Insider optional

Off by default; for 3+ players, ages 12+ (never a kid player). One adult is secretly working against the hall. In secret votes they get a quiet sabotage option in their private note, up to twice. At the end everyone accuses someone; evidence found during the game rules people out. The Insider wins if the town survives and they aren't named.

📚 Campaign: The Kelderwick Files

Three linked episodes of about 30 minutes each, with the same household: Blackout (November), The Water Week (January) and Who Had the Key? (March). Start it from 📚 Campaign on the title screen. Choices carry over (was Mr Aldous saved? were the stores opened?) and each episode opens with “Previously on Day Zero…”. Episode 3 ends with the Inquiry. Progress is saved on this device; copy the campaign code to move it to another one. Score: up to 27 ⭐.

🏆 How to win

There's no single win or lose; the Chronicle gives you up to 9 stars in three categories:

Stars★★★★★★
Mission: the HubWarmth, Water and Information all runningTwo of themOne or none
People: Health5 or more3–41–2
Community: Cohesion + Trust8 or more5–74 or less

8–9 stars earns the best ending, The Lights of Mill Lane. If Health or Cohesion drops to 0, the game ends early. Afterwards, “Show what you missed” reveals which rumours were true.
